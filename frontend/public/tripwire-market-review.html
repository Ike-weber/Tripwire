Is anyone already solving this?

Yes — and knowing exactly who changes how Tripwire should be pitched.

Why this doc exists Tripwire's own proposal names Forta, BlockSec, and Harpie as the landscape. That list is incomplete and understates how far incumbents have moved. This review checks the real 2025–2026 competitive picture — Forta Firewall, Blockaid, Hypernative, and Safe's own native Shield — and reframes where Tripwire's actual, defensible edge is.

01 · Is the problem real

The demand isn't hypothetical

Wallet-drainer losses fell sharply from their 2024 peak, but the underlying attack pattern — tricking a signer into an approval, not stealing a key — is still today's dominant threat, and 2026's worst incidents are passing every formal security check at the moment of execution.

02 · Who else is building this

The real competitive set

Four organizations matter here. Two protect protocols. One protects wallets at the platform level, and it's the platform Tripwire is built on.

Forta Firewalla16z / Coinbase Ventures · market standard, 2025
Screened 700M+ transactions in 2025 alone, with days exceeding 8M. Sub-10ms latency, >99% exploit-detection recall, RPC-level pre-block screening. Adopted at the chain level — Kraken's Ink, Celo, Plume — and by Compound, Lido, MakerDAO.
Where it doesn't compete with Tripwire: protocol/chain-side by design — the target contract or chain has to integrate it. A victim's own wallet is unprotected against a malicious contract that never opted in.
BlockaidSeries B, $50M · Tel Aviv, founded 2022
Powers the transaction simulation and security alerts built into several major wallets already — most people who've seen a "this transaction looks risky" warning in a mainstream wallet have likely seen Blockaid's engine at work.
Where it doesn't compete with Tripwire: alerts and simulates; doesn't itself hold enforcement authority over the wallet's execution path — that decision stays with the wallet vendor integrating it.
HypernativeSeries B, $40M · powers Safe Shield's enterprise tier
300+ dApps, chains, wallets, and financial institutions rely on it for real-time threat detection and automated incident response. This is the engine behind Safe Shield's paid enforcement layer — automatic blocking, custom policy rules, Transaction Guards — shipping today, on the exact wallet standard Tripwire targets.
Where it doesn't compete with Tripwire: gated behind Safe's enterprise/paid tier, aimed at organizations needing enforcement "at scale" — not self-hostable, not aimed at individuals or small DAOs configuring their own policy.
HarpieShut down, 2025
Billed as the first on-chain firewall protecting individual wallets from hacks and theft — the closest historical analog to Tripwire's original "protect the person, not the protocol" framing.
Why it matters anyway: it shut down on unit economics, not because wallet-side protection doesn't work technically. Proof of demand, and a warning about the business model, not the architecture.

03 · What's crowded, what's open

Where Tripwire can still stand

  • Crowded
    "We detect and block malicious transactions"Forta, Blockaid, and Hypernative all do this, at production scale, with venture-scale funding behind each.
  • Crowded
    "We add enforcement to Safe via a Guard module"Safe Shield already ships exactly this, natively, with Hypernative as the enforcement partner — as of November 2025.
  • Open
    Self-hostable, individually configurable enforcementHypernative's Safe integration is aimed at organizations needing enforcement "at scale." Nothing found targets a single power-user or a small DAO who wants to run their own policy layer without an enterprise contract.
  • Open
    Natural-language policy configuration"Only auto-approve sends under $500 to addresses I've paid before, delay everything else an hour" parsed straight into a Guard's config struct. Not found in any competitor's public feature set.
  • Open
    Transparent, auditable reasoning trail per verdictIncumbents largely treat scoring as a black box behind an API. An open RiskRegistry with a plain-English reason attached to every verdict is a legible, inspectable alternative — a real trust argument for a skeptical DAO signer group.

04 · Reframing the pitch

From "we solve wallet drainers" to something defensible

The original pitch — "protect the wallet, not the protocol" — is no longer differentiated on its own; Safe Shield does exactly that today. The version worth pitching is narrower and truer:

Tripwire is an open, self-hostable enforcement layer for the wallets and DAOs that enterprise security platforms don't prioritize — configured in plain language, not a policy console.

Concretely, that means naming Forta, Blockaid, and Hypernative directly in the pitch rather than omitting them, and making the claim: what Hypernative validated at the enterprise tier, we're proving works open and self-configurable for everyone else. That's a smaller claim than the original, but it's one nobody in this review is already making — which makes it far more credible to a judge who knows this space.

05 · Bottom line

Verdict

Net assessment The problem is real and current — losses are still material and 2026's worst incidents are bypassing formal checks entirely. But the core "detect and enforce" loop is no longer a gap: Forta owns protocol-side screening, Blockaid is inside major wallets already, and Hypernative already powers native enforcement on Safe itself. Tripwire's technical design is sound and its fail-closed discipline is genuinely good — but its differentiation has to move from detection and enforcement (taken) to open, configurable, natural-language policy for the segment incumbents don't serve (still open). That's a smaller, more honest claim — and a stronger one to defend in front of a judge who's done this same research.
